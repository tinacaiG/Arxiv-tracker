Topology-Inspired Fine-Tuning for Wind-Field Super-Resolution
Authors: Arjun Dadhwal, Brian Summa
First: 2026-10-15T17:28:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:28:39+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 6 figures; extended version of a poster accepted at TopoInVis Connect 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Scientific super-resolution (SR) models are typically optimized with pointwise reconstruction losses, yet scientific interpretation often hinges on extrema, connected regions, and multiscale scalar structure. We characterize this tension with pretrained CNN and GAN wind-field SR models. Pointwise metrics, bottleneck persistence-diagram (PD) distance, and merge-tree (MT) distance favor the CNN, whereas order-2 Wasserstein PD distances favor the sharper GAN, so even different distances on the same topological descriptor reward different properties. Motivated by this disagreement, we fine-tune the pretrained CNN with differentiable topology-inspired auxiliary losses on scalar speed, gradient magnitude, soft high-speed level sets, and local maxima, and with fixed-pair supervision, which constrains predicted values and persistence at ground-truth persistence-pair endpoints located offline with the Topology ToolKit. A loss study of 16 fine-tuning configurations, each compared with a matched standard fine-tuning control that uses only the original reconstruction loss, on a fixed 168-field benchmark (with topology evaluated on a fixed 160 by 160 subregion of each field), shows that, among the scalar-field losses, gradient supervision provides the strongest PD improvement but does not improve mean MT distance, whereas fixed-pair supervision gives the largest and most consistent joint improvement in PD and MT distances. The final objective combines these two signals. Relative to the pretrained CNN, it lowers mean bottleneck, $W_{2,\infty}$, and $W_{2,2}$ distances by 31%, 25%, and 27% (and by 32-35% relative to the control, which on its own worsens all three), lowers all three on 150 of 168 fields, and gains 1.30 dB PSNR, about half the gain of standard fine-tuning. Mean MT distance also improves, but on only 91 of those 150 fields, indicating that PD and MT capture complementary structure.

SR-Prominence: A Crowdsourced Protocol and Dataset Suite for Perceptually-Weighted Super-Resolution Artifact Evaluation

Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-05-14T13:56:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T11:54:08+00:00
Comments: accepted to NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets track
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Modern image super-resolution methods generate detailed, visually appealing results, but they often introduce visual artifacts: unnatural patterns and texture distortions that degrade perceived quality. These defects vary widely in perceptual impact--some are barely noticeable, while others are highly disturbing--yet existing detection methods treat them equally. We propose artifact prominence as an evaluative target, defined as the fraction of viewers who judge a highlighted region to contain a noticeable artifact. We design a crowdsourced annotation protocol and construct SR-Prominence, a dataset suite containing 3,935 artifact masks from DeSRA, Open Images, Urban100, and a realistic no-ground-truth Urban100-HR setting, annotated with prominence. Re-annotating DeSRA reveals that 48.2% of its in-lab binary artifacts are not noticed by a majority of viewers. Across the suite, we audit SR artifact detectors, image-quality metrics, and SR methods. We find that classical full-reference metrics, especially SSIM and DISTS, provide surprisingly strong localized prominence signals, whereas no-reference IQA methods and specialized artifact detectors often fail to generalize across datasets and reference settings. SR-Prominence is released with an objective scoring protocol that allows new metrics to be benchmarked on our suite without further crowdsourcing. Together, the data and protocols enable SR artifact evaluation to move from binary defect presence toward perceptual impact. SR-Prominence is available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/imolodetskikh/sr-artifact-prominence.

Abstract
Interest in generating realistic synthetic medical images has grown in recent years. Such datasets can mitigate the scarcity of public data for artificial intelligence research and support education. Work on synthesizing dental panoramic radiographs (PRs) has so far relied almost exclusively on generative adversarial networks (GANs), and three limitations recur: the synthesized field of view is often restricted to a cropped dentoalveolar region rather than a complete PR; observer studies of GAN-generated PRs report that clinicians still identify synthetic images with accuracies of roughly 78-82%, implying persistent visible giveaways; and adversarial training remains sensitive to instability and mode collapse. Generating a full-size PR directly at diagnostic resolution with a diffusion model is an alternative, but sampling at that resolution is costly. We therefore present PanoDiff-SR, an end-to-end two-stage pipeline developed and evaluated specifically for panoramic radiography: a denoising diffusion implicit model (PanoDiff) generates a low-resolution (LR) seed PR ($256\times128$), which a fine-tuned hybrid attention transformer (HAT) super-resolution (SR) stage upscales to a high-resolution (HR) PR of $1024\times512$. Decoupling the expensive generative step from the resolution step yields complete, full-arch PRs at diagnostic resolution on a single 47 GB GPU. The Fréchet inception distance between 7243 real and synthetic HR images is 40.5. Inception scores were 2.68, 2.38, 2.90 and 2.98 for real HR, synthetic HR, real LR and synthetic LR images, respectively. Six clinical experts, each evaluating 100 synthetic and 100 real PRs under a time limit, distinguished real from synthetic images with an average accuracy of 68.5% (50% corresponds to random guessing), substantially closer to chance than reported for GAN-based studies. VascuSR: Iterative Anatomy-Aware Diffusion for CT Super-Resolution
Authors: Hairong Ni, Jiahe Huang, Minghao Guo, Wojciech Matusik, Rose Yu
First: 2026-10-14T17:12:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:12:56+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Generative super-resolution (SR) can recover sharp detail in computed tomography (CT) images from severely downsampled observations, but the missing high-frequency content is not uniquely determined by the low-resolution input. Consequently, visually plausible reconstructions may contain anatomically unsupported vascular detail, such as missing, spurious, or incorrectly connected vessels. To overcome this challenge, we introduce VascuSR, an iterative anatomy-aware diffusion framework that combines image reconstruction with explicit 3D vascular guidance. First, to provide richer anatomical guidance, we use an explicit 3D vascular representation that captures structural information beyond individual slices. This representation also simplifies vascular refinement by allowing a lightweight 3D generative refiner to focus on vascular structure rather than complex CT appearance. Second, to guide slice-wise SR, a lightweight anatomy controller injects the refined structure into the diffusion decoder, while iterative feedback updates this guidance from each new SR volume. On re-annotated MSD Task08 and HVM datasets, VascuSR outperforms interpolation and learning-based SR baselines in segmentation accuracy (Dice and clDice) and 3D vascular structure (connectivity and mean centerline distance), while maintaining image quality comparable to its SR backbone. Compared with learning-based baselines, VascuSR reduces class-averaged connected-component counts by over 20% on both datasets and mean centerline distance by over 10% on MSD.

Extreme-Scale Super-Resolution of Homogeneous Turbulence from a Single Realization
Authors: Arth Sojitra, Muhammad Abid, Omer San
First: 2026-10-14T14:54:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T14:54:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Homogeneous turbulent flows exhibit recurring multiscale structures across spatially separated regions, suggesting that a single flow realization may contain sufficient information to learn representations of unresolved scales. We investigate this hypothesis through an extreme super-resolution framework in which a wide-activation deep super-resolution (WDSR) network is trained exclusively on overlapping patches extracted from a single high-resolution turbulent realization. Previously unseen flow fields are reconstructed through patchwise prediction followed by Hann-window-weighted overlap-add assembly. The framework is evaluated for two-dimensional homogeneous isotropic turbulence at super-resolution factors of x32 and x64, representing severe spatial under-resolution. Despite the limited training data, the proposed approach reconstructs coherent vortical structures and recovers fine-scale features more accurately than conventional interpolation. Spectral and statistical analyses further demonstrate improved recovery of high-wavenumber energy content and multiscale flow characteristics. To assess the effectiveness of exploiting internal spatial information, we compare the proposed framework against WDSR models trained on 1, 25, 50, and 100 complete high-resolution snapshots without patch extraction. This comparison distinguishes the benefits of localized learning from those associated with increasing the number of training realizations. The results demonstrate that exploiting spatial redundancy within a single turbulent realization enables accurate reconstruction of previously unseen flow fields, even under extreme spatial under-resolution. These findings establish single-realization patch-based learning as a promising data-efficient approach for turbulent super-resolution when high-resolution training data are scarce.

BridgeTwist: Twisting Schrödinger Bridges for Training-Free Conditional Sampling
Authors: Mengyu Li, Qianqian Qu, Jun S. Liu
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-13T16:20:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T16:20:49+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Generative models are often required to serve many downstream conditional tasks. In the training-free setting, a pretrained unconditional model is reused as a prior and combined with a task-specific likelihood to sample from the corresponding posterior. While such training-free samplers have been developed for diffusion and flow models, their counterpart for Schrödinger bridge (SB) models remains unexplored, despite the growing appeal of SBs as flexible stochastic transport models between general source and target distributions. We propose BridgeTwist, a training-free conditional sampler for pretrained SB generative models, formulated as a tempered twisted sequential Monte Carlo scheme. Its twist is a closed-form plug-in surrogate, obtained by combining the velocity and score fields exposed by SB pretraining into an endpoint predictor and evaluating the likelihood there. Since this surrogate is less reliable near the source, we temper the twist with a time-varying exponent, downweighting its influence at early steps. We further introduce a bridge-shaped inference-time diffusion schedule that concentrates the proposal noise in the interior of the path. Telescoping importance weights then cancel all intermediate twist factors, yielding an asymptotically exact sampler for the posterior. Empirically, BridgeTwist outperforms training-free baselines on class-conditional and text-prompted image generation, and is competitive with specialized inverse problem solvers on inpainting, deblurring, and super-resolution.

Generative models are often required to serve many downstream conditional tasks.
InstantViR: Real-Time Video Inverse Problem Solver with Distilled Diffusion Prior
Authors: Weimin Bai, Suzhe Xu, Yiwei Ren, Jinhua Hao, Ming Sun, Wenzheng Chen, He Sun
First: 2025-11-18T07:40:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T09:40:29+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Video inverse problems are fundamental to streaming, telepresence, and AR/VR, where high perceptual quality must coexist with tight latency constraints. Diffusion-based priors currently deliver state-of-the-art reconstructions, but existing approaches either adapt image diffusion models with ad hoc temporal regularizers - leading to temporal artifacts - or rely on native video diffusion models whose iterative posterior sampling is far too slow for real-time use. We introduce InstantViR, an amortized inference framework for ultra-fast video reconstruction powered by a pre-trained video diffusion prior. We distill a powerful bidirectional video diffusion model (teacher) into a causal autoregressive student that maps a degraded video directly to its restored version in a single forward pass, inheriting the teacher's strong temporal modeling while completely removing iterative test-time optimization. The distillation is prior-driven: it only requires the teacher diffusion model and known degradation operators, and does not rely on externally paired clean/noisy video data. To further boost throughput, we replace the video-diffusion backbone VAE with a high-efficiency LeanVAE via an innovative teacher-space regularized distillation scheme, enabling low-latency latent-space processing. Across streaming random inpainting, Gaussian deblurring and super-resolution, InstantViR matches or surpasses the reconstruction quality of diffusion-based baselines while running at over 35 FPS on NVIDIA A100 GPUs, achieving up to 100 times speedups over iterative video diffusion solvers. These results show that diffusion-based video reconstruction is compatible with real-time, interactive, editable, streaming scenarios, turning high-quality video restoration into a practical component of modern vision systems.

Abstract
High-fidelity three-dimensional turbulent flow fields are essential for scientific and engineering analyses, yet obtaining them through direct numerical simulation or high-resolution measurements remains expensive. Super-resolution reconstruction from coarse-grained data therefore offers a promising alternative, but remains fundamentally challenging because fine-scale structures are intrinsically three-dimensional and dynamically coupled across scales. Existing data-driven approaches still struggle to recover multiscale volumetric structures and their coherent temporal evolution. To address these limitations, we propose a turbulence-aware framework for time-resolved three-dimensional flow reconstruction. The framework couples a multiscale neural operator, designed to recover volumetric structures, with a state space model to promote temporal consistency in the reconstructed dynamics. In addition, Navier-Stokes residuals are incorporated as soft constraints to regularize the reconstruction toward physically plausible dynamics. A mixture-of-experts mechanism is also introduced to support unsupervised adaptation to unknown coarse-graining. Across evaluations on canonical turbulent systems and atmospheric reanalysis data, the proposed framework reduces reconstruction errors relative to learning-based and classical baselines, while better preserving multiscale spatial structure and coherent temporal evolution. These results validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in reconstructing high-fidelity three-dimensional flow fields from coarse-grained data.

Abstract
While recent autoregressive video diffusion models achieve remarkable streaming quality, they remain confined to low resolutions (e.g., 480P), leaving efficient, scalable, real-time high-resolution video generation a fundamental open challenge. Abstract
Recent video super-resolution (VSR) models achieve efficient one-step generation through diffusion distillation. However, high-resolution inference remains costly, and distilled models are difficult to align with human preferences. These challenges call for improvements in both model architecture and training strategy. We present \textbf{FlashVSR+} to address both limitations. On the architecture side, we introduce hybrid attention based on the strong locality observed in pretrained VSR models, while retaining sparse global interaction for long-range consistency. It achieves up to $2\times$ DiT speedup and 45\% lower peak memory, enabling spatially untiled 4K VSR on a single 24GB RTX 4090. Ry-DMD uses reward-weighted teacher rollouts to incorporate black-box rewards into distribution matching, without backpropagating through the reward model. We further show that Ry-DMD generalizes beyond VSR to T2I generation. Together, FlashVSR+ delivers stronger restoration quality and substantially improves high-resolution efficiency, with consistent gains in user preference. Hardware-Friendly Token-Group Activation Quantization for Low-Bit Mamba Super-Resolution
Authors: Jinwoo Chung, Jangho Kim

First: 2026-10-12T06:43:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-12T06:43:58+00:00

Abstract
Mamba-based super-resolution models are efficient alternatives to Transformer-based restoration backbones, but their low-bit post-training quantization remains challenging because selective scan, Mamba's input-dependent state-space operator, induces different activation ranges across spatial tokens. Thus, a single layer-wise clipping range poorly matches all tokens. We propose Token-Group Quantization, a hardware-friendly activation PTQ framework that statically approximates a hardware-unfriendly post-scan grouping oracle, where tokens with similar preferred clipping ranges are assigned to the same group. To avoid runtime post-scan grouping in static low-bit inference, a lightweight predictor infers the groups from pre-scan features available before the quantized scan path. During calibration, we form pseudo-labels by clustering post-scan activation descriptors that capture similar rounding--clipping behavior. At inference, the frozen predictor maps pre-scan features to groups and selects entries from offline-calibrated group-wise clipping-bound tables, without runtime statistics collection, dynamic clipping updates, or post-scan group assignment. To address low-bit restoration degradation, we further introduce a Frequency-Preserving Refinement (FPR) objective for clipping-bound calibration. FPR aligns Fourier amplitude and phase between full-precision teacher and quantized student outputs to preserve high-frequency structures. Across PTQ protocols, bit-widths, backbones, and tasks, our method consistently outperforms prior Mamba and SR PTQ baselines, transfers to Transformer SR and Vision Mamba classification, composes with rotation-based quantization, and achieves 2.23x speedup on Jetson Orin Nano over FP16. GENERIC-FNO: Embedding Energy Conservation and Entropy Production into Fourier Neural Operators
Authors: Jason Sulskis, Sathya Ravi
First: 2026-06-06T21:24:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-12T00:26:37+00:00
Comments: Under review
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We propose GENERIC-FNO, a neural operator that embeds the metriplectic (GENERIC) degeneracy structure of nonequilibrium thermodynamics in function space, coupling reversible, energy-conserving dynamics to irreversible, entropy-producing dynamics via the degeneracy conditions. Prior structure-preserving neural operators enforce at most one conservation law or a Hamiltonian form, and thermodynamically consistent learning has been confined to finite-dimensional, graph, or particle systems. GENERIC-FNO learns the energy and entropy functionals as neural operators and builds the reversible and irreversible operators as diagonal Fourier multipliers flanked by rank-one projections that enforce both degeneracy conditions exactly, by construction, with no penalty, update projection, or residual; the Jacobi identity is not enforced. The identities hold to machine precision (~10^-13) for any initialization, dimension, or resolution, so the continuous-time dynamics conserve the learned energy and produce the learned entropy exactly, with explicit time stepping adding only an O(dt^2) drift. These are guarantees about the learned functionals within GENERIC's scope of closed conservative-dissipative dynamics, not a certificate of physical accuracy, and the (E,S,L,M) decomposition is not unique; we make this gauge freedom explicit and propose a gauge-invariant dissipation diagnostic independent of the learned functionals. Across three backbones (1D/2D FNO, DeepONet) and four canonical scalar PDEs, the guarantees transfer zero-shot over a 4x super-resolution range and hold in 3D; the diagnostic identifies the reversible and the most dissipative system in every backbone; and over 200-step rollouts, where every unconstrained model we test diverges or collapses, GENERIC-FNO stays bounded, at half the parameters but 4-10x the compute, while losing accuracy on pure transport and on the smallest 1D backbone.

Abstract
Plasma experiments observe coupled physical states through sparse, heterogeneous, and incomplete diagnostics. We present a proof of concept toward a multimodal foundation model for plasma state reconstruction during magnetic reconnection, motivated by the Facility for Laboratory Reconnection Experiments (FLARE). Using 250 synthetic VPIC simulations at fixed reference electron beta $β_{e0}=0.2$, with 225 training runs and 25 disjoint validation runs, we train a 5.5M-parameter residual 3D U-Net to reconstruct three magnetic-field components and electron density from incomplete observations. The model combines spatial-only pooling, which preserves all 24 temporal frames, with global spatiotemporal self-attention and rotary positional encoding. Independently masking magnetic and density modalities across random, grid, block, and temporal patterns enables sparse sensing, cross-modal completion, density super-resolution, and temporal completion with a single set of weights and no task-specific retraining. Conversely, observed density improves current-density reconstruction when magnetic data are absent or extremely sparse. With no magnetic observations, roughly 1% density visibility approaches the density reconstruction accuracy achieved with fully observed magnetic fields, although current structure remains more dependent on direct magnetic measurements. Full magnetic conditioning also stabilizes completion of missing future density frames. These results demonstrate bidirectional cross-modal predictive coupling within this simulation regime. Generalization across plasma regimes or to experimental measurements remains untested.

DuSRFlow: Dual-Lens Super-Resolution via Parallax-Aware Flow Estimation and Complementary Reference Warping
Authors: Fangpu Zhang, Huanjing Yue, Zifan Cui, Shaoxiong Tu, Qirui Yang, Jingyu Yang

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Dual-lens super-resolution (DuSR) aims to enhance a wide-angle low-resolution (LR) image by exploiting a telephoto reference (Ref) image. A central challenge in DuSR is reliable reference utilization under large parallax, occlusion, and cross-camera imaging discrepancies. Existing methods often employ off-the-shelf monocular optical flow models to provide geometric priors. However, their assumptions of photometric consistency and smooth motion are violated in dual-lens scenarios, leading to unreliable alignment and degraded reference-based reconstruction. To address these issues, we propose DuSRFlow, a dual-lens super-resolution framework that improves reference utilization from both correspondence learning and reference aggregation. First, we introduce DuFlowNet, a dedicated dual-lens flow estimator for robust cross-view alignment. To train DuFlowNet without ground-truth flow annotations, we develop a real--synthetic data fusion strategy that composites synthetic foreground objects onto real dual-lens image pairs, generating controllable large-parallax samples while preserving realistic cross-camera imaging characteristics. Second, we observe that reference reliability is highly spatially uneven in DuSR, where unreliable local reference responses may suppress the utilization of reliable cues. We therefore propose a Complementary Reference Warping strategy that coordinates patch-based warping and kernel-free-guided deformable warping according to local reference reliability, producing a more reliable reference representation before LR--Ref fusion. In addition, we construct DuSR-RealV2, a challenging real-world benchmark with complex depth variations, large parallax, and occlusion. Extensive experiments on multiple DuSR benchmarks demonstrate that DuSRFlow achieves state-of-the-art performance in reconstruction quality, perceptual quality, and cross-dataset generalization.

Abstract
Diffusion models have achieved remarkable success in image super-resolution (SR) by leveraging strong generative priors to enhance perceptual quality. However, for textual content, these priors often produce text hallucinations: non-existent or distorted text, which alter scene semantics and compromise model reliability. While recent methods focus on enhancing text generation, they do so indiscriminately, leaving hallucinations fundamentally unresolved. We argue the core problem is a lack of selective control. TGSR consistently improves text fidelity across multiple backbones, increasing recognition precision by up to 17.68 points without degrading perceptual quality, underscoring the value of selective control in generative SR.

Abstract
Pretrained diffusion Transformers provide powerful generative priors for image restoration, yet adapting them to structure-sensitive tasks remains challenging. Existing methods mainly rely on dense low-resolution features as conditions, which often entangle salient objects with dominant backgrounds and provide limited abstraction for recurring structures across scales and samples. We propose MOSAIC, an object-centric representation learning framework that learns compact structural conditions for generative model adaptation. MOSAIC organizes degraded observations into structural slots that capture complementary object-level and layout-level evidence, integrates them across resolutions, and retrieves recurring priors from a shared prototype memory to complement incomplete image-specific information. Abstract
In an image latent space, the embeddings of high-resolution, natural, and sharp images form a manifold. Degradation of high-resolution images pushes their embeddings off this manifold. Real-world super-resolution (SR) then becomes the task of mapping the degraded embedding back onto this manifold --- not anywhere on the manifold, but to the point that preserves what the input still carries, both its semantics and pixel details.
