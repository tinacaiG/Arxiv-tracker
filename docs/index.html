GigaBench: Benchmarking Learnable Representations for Gigapixel Image Compression and Super-Resolution
Authors: Matteo Fusconi, Alex Costanzino, Pierluigi Zama Ramirez, Luigi Di Stefano
First: 2026-10-22T17:55:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:55:10+00:00
Comments: Project page at https://matteofusconi.github.io/GigaBench Accepted at WACV2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Learnable Representations (LRs) can fit parametric families of functions to diverse kinds of signals. These emergent neural-native data formats are gathering interest in the scientific community due to their compactness, resolution-agnosticism and amenability to direct neural processing. On the other hand, gigapixel images are becoming increasingly widespread across a variety of scientific, cultural, and industrial domains. In this paper, we present GigaBench, the first benchmark and experimental evaluation focused on deploying LRs with gigapixel imagery. GigaBench includes images collected, acquired, and synthetically rendered across diverse domains, and addresses image compression and super-resolution. We adapt a representative set of LRs, spanning Implicit, Hybrid, and Explicit methods, so as to operate reliably at the gigapixel scale on consumer hardware. Through systematic evaluation across reconstruction quality, model size, compression and inference times, we identify consistent trends across methods and suggest actionable guidance for future investigation.

Squaring-the-Sphere: Distortion-Free and Seamless Panorama Generation via CubeAtlas
Authors: Xuhui Zuo, Chenjie Cao, Tengfei Wang, Junta Wu, Yisu Zhang, Chunchao Guo
First: 2026-10-22T17:06:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:06:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Generating 360-degree panoramas requires both local perspective fidelity and global spherical continuity. Existing representations face a trade-off: equirectangular projection (ERP) enables seamless panorama modeling but suffers from severe distortion and a domain gap to pre-trained image generators, whereas cubemaps preserve perspective geometry but often produce inconsistent faces and visible seams. We present Squaring-the-Sphere, a panoramic image generation framework built on a novel representation CubeAtlas, which unfolds all cubemap faces into a single compact canvas. By arranging orientation-aligned, rotated polar regions alongside the equatorial faces, this representation preserves the perspective geometry of cubemaps while organizing the cross-face relationships required for seamless panorama assembly. It further enables a pretrained diffusion transformer to model the complete panorama jointly in a single pass, without altering the backbone architecture or positional encoding. While CubeAtlas has a token count comparable to ERP, its structured layout makes many dense attention interactions unnecessary. We therefore introduce a layout-aware sparse attention mask that preserves the interactions required for panorama continuity while removing geometrically irrelevant token pairs. As a result, CubeAtlas achieves better quality than ERP modeling with lower attention computation. Furthermore, we introduce a training-free super-resolution technique and a fusion scheme that enhance fine details in distortion-free perspective space before achieving the final ERP panorama. Experiments on various benchmarks demonstrate that our method improves geometric fidelity, cross-view consistency, and high-resolution visual quality over previous methods.

Generating 360-degree panoramas requires both local perspective fidelity and global spherical continuity.
Noise-Started One-Step Real-World Super-Resolution via LR-Conditioned SplitMeanFlow and GAN Refinement
Authors: Wei Zhu, Kai Zhang, Yu Zheng, Lei Luo, Yong Guo, Jian Yang
First: 2026-05-10T05:01:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T00:56:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Pre-trained text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models have shown strong potential for real-world image super-resolution (Real-ISR), owing to their noise-started generation process that enables realistic texture synthesis and captures the one-to-many nature of super-resolution. However, diffusion-based Real-ISR methods still face a fundamental efficiency-quality trade-off. Multi-step methods generate high-quality results by iteratively denoising random Gaussian noise under LR conditioning, but suffer from slow sampling. Recent one-step methods greatly improve efficiency, yet they typically replace noise-started generation with direct LR-to-HR restoration, which weakens stochasticity and limits realistic detail synthesis. To address this issue, we propose SMFSR, a noise-started one-step Real-ISR framework via LR-conditioned SplitMeanFlow and GAN refinement. SMFSR preserves the random-noise starting point of diffusion models and learns a direct noise-to-HR mapping conditioned on the LR image. To this end, Interval Splitting Consistency distills the multi-step generative trajectory into a single average-velocity prediction, enabling efficient one-step generation. To compensate for the reduced opportunity for progressive refinement, we further introduce a GAN refinement stage, where a DINOv3-based discriminator enhances realistic texture synthesis and variational score distillation aligns the generated outputs with the natural image distribution under a frozen diffusion teacher. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SMFSR achieves state-of-the-art perceptual quality among one-step diffusion-based Real-ISR methods while retaining fast single-step inference.

Decoupled Data Consistency with Diffusion Purification for Image Restoration
Authors: Xiang Li, Soo Min Kwon, Shijun Liang, Ismail R. Alkhouri, Saiprasad Ravishankar, Qing Qu
First: 2024-03-10T00:47:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T21:52:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Diffusion models have recently gained traction as a powerful class of deep generative priors, excelling in a wide range of image restoration tasks due to their exceptional ability to model data distributions. To solve image restoration problems, many existing techniques achieve data consistency by incorporating additional likelihood gradient steps into the reverse sampling process of diffusion models. However, the additional gradient steps pose a challenge for real-world practical applications as they incur a large computational overhead, thereby increasing inference time. They also present additional difficulties when using accelerated diffusion model samplers, as the number of data consistency steps is limited by the number of reverse sampling steps. In this work, we propose a novel diffusion-based image restoration solver that addresses these issues by decoupling the reverse process from the data consistency steps. Our method involves alternating between a reconstruction phase to maintain data consistency and a refinement phase that enforces the prior via diffusion purification. Our approach demonstrates versatility, making it highly adaptable for efficient problem-solving in latent space. Additionally, it reduces the necessity for numerous sampling steps through the integration of consistency models. The efficacy of our approach is validated through comprehensive experiments across various image restoration tasks, including image denoising, deblurring, inpainting, and super-resolution.

Arbitrary-Scale Anisotropic 3D MRI Super-Resolution with Gaussian Prototypes
Authors: Aditya Rastogi, Ramon Leiser, Prateek Singh, Martha Foltyn-Dumitru, Martin Bendszus, Fabian Bamberg, Klaus Maier-Hein, Kai Schlamp, Alexander Radbruch, Gianluca Brugnara, Philipp Vollmuth

Comments: 26 pages, 6 figures, 15 tables (including appendix)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reducing MRI acquisition time improves patient comfort, reduces motion artefacts, and increases scanner throughput. Clinical protocols often shorten acquisitions by sacrificing spatial resolution, particularly along the slice axis. Super-resolution can recover higher-resolution images, but existing methods are typically specialised to one scale or acquisition geometry, limiting their use across heterogeneous clinical protocols. We present GSMR3D, a continuous volumetric model that reconstructs arbitrary isotropic and anisotropic resolutions with a single checkpoint per MRI contrast. It combines a learned dictionary of anisotropic 3D Gaussian prototypes with scale-conditioned frequency encoding and an exact measurement-consistency projection, keeping reconstructions consistent with the acquired low-resolution data. We train separate models on 3,731 contrast-enhanced T1-weighted and 30,254 T1-weighted brain MRI volumes from clinical and population-based cohorts. We evaluate GSMR3D against geometry-specific specialists for isotropic, in-plane, and through-plane super-resolution using reconstruction quality and downstream tasks: brain tumour segmentation, brain-metastasis detection, and brain parcellation. GSMR3D outperforms specialists at isotropic $2\times$ and achieves better LPIPS, DISTS, and FID across all geometries, although specialists retain higher PSNR in anisotropic settings. It more faithfully preserves glioma segmentations across all geometries, achieves the highest lesion sensitivity on an independent brain-metastasis cohort, and the highest brain-parcellation agreement for isotropic and in-plane reconstruction. Reconstruction takes about 6s per volume, adding little overhead relative to acquisition-time savings. GSMR3D offers a flexible approach to MRI scan-time reduction with one model per contrast across geometries while preserving information relevant to downstream tasks.

WorldSR: Harnessing World Knowledge Search for Grounded Image Super-Resolution
Authors: Junyuan Deng, Xinyi Wu, Zhenyao Wu, Song Wang, Yu Wang, Congchao Zhu
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-20T15:54:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T15:54:25+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Project Page: https://gwanjyun.github.io/worldsr.github.io/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Generative-prior-based super-resolution (SR) methods leverage implicit knowledge learned from large-scale models to enhance low-resolution inputs. However, such priors are inherently stochastic and optimized for diverse generation, conflicting with the deterministic, high-fidelity reconstruction required for SR. It is also tied to the base model, costly to update, and prone to hallucinations as real-world data evolves. To address these limitations, we introduce WorldSR, a novel framework that augments SR with explicit, entity-aligned visual world knowledge. WorldSR automatically decomposes the input into semantic entities and performs entity-wise retrieval of structurally and semantically aligned images from a large-scale external corpus. For efficiency and reliability, we incorporate a memory mechanism to reuse previously retrieved results and a multi-step filtering process to refine candidate references. The selected references as world knowledge are then integrated through a novel multi-reference super-resolution model, which provides fine-grained constraints for detail synthesis and structural recovery. Experiments on real-world SR benchmarks show that WorldSR consistently improves structural fidelity and visual quality, outperforming existing generative super-resolution methods. Our results suggest that entity-level grounding in external visual world knowledge provides a simple and effective complement to model-bound generative priors.

Abstract
Diffusion magnetic resonance imaging is a noninvasive technique that is sensitive to the motion of water molecules in human tissue. The image signal is commonly described in q-space; however, a complete coverage of this space is often infeasible due to hardware and acquisition time limitations. In this work, we propose to represent the entire diffusion signal using an implicit neural representation network with wavelet activation functions. The signal space is encoded using Fourier features and spherical harmonics basis functions for a continuous output distribution of the raw diffusion data, enabling both super-resolution and q-space interpolation. Additionally, we introduce a pretraining strategy using a group template, enabling faster per-subject optimisation when combined with nonlinear registrations of subjects into template space. We tested the method with 10 subjects from the Human Connectome Project and 26 subjects from in-house data (multidimensional diffusion MRI from a Parkinson's disease cohort). The results show that the proposed method yields high-fidelity reconstructions with significantly reduced memory requirements compared to raw diffusion-weighted images. The pretraining scheme showed good generalisability properties on data acquired with very different protocols. We further assess the method on downstream metrics relevant to diffusion MRI data from the learned representations.

Scalable Stress-Constrained Topology Optimization: A Super-resolution and Simultaneous Approach Based on Machine Learning
Authors: Xiangyu Sun, Shirin Hosseinmardi, Ramin Bostanabad
First: 2026-10-20T12:52:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T12:52:59+00:00
Comments: 40 pages, 19 figures, journal paper
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large-scale topology optimization (TO) remains computationally demanding, especially for stress-constrained problems. To address this challenge, we propose the simultaneous neural topology optimization (SNTO) framework where we represent state, adjoint, and design variables with custom neural networks (NNs) whose parameters are jointly optimized within a single optimization loop that leverages continuous adjoint analysis and automatic differentiation. This optimization minimizes a multi-component loss function that combines the design objective, an aggregation of the local stress constraints, and the potential energy functionals. Our NNs have a unique architecture that accelerates convergence while enabling the networks to capture sharp gradients and boundaries which are critical to stress-constrained TO. To achieve scalability to millions of degrees of freedom (DoF), we develop a hardware-aware training scheme in which the design domain is partitioned into subdomains that are distributed across multiple GPUs. To control peak GPU memory usage, we further slice each subdomain into smaller regions whose loss gradients are evaluated sequentially and accumulated to update the NN parameters. Unlike domain decomposition approaches, our partitioning naturally ensures field continuity across the subdomains and does not require the introduction of additional terms into the loss function. For validation, we first evaluate the proposed framework on various 2D stress-constrained benchmarks and compare our results to a conventional finite element-based approach, showing that SNTO produces super-resolution designs with sharper boundaries, smaller gray areas, and lower volume fractions. We then consider large-scale 3D problems with up to eight million collocation points and distribute the training over up to eight GPUs.

Abstract
Real-world image super-resolution aims to recover high-quality images from complex and unknown real-world degradations. However, existing generative Real-ISR methods largely inherit the dense latent representations and quadratic-cost global modeling paradigm developed for high-resolution image synthesis, causing computation, memory usage, and inference latency to scale unfavorably with resolution and thus limiting practical deployment. We argue that the key bottleneck lies not in insufficient restoration priors, but in excessive token redundancy and costly token interactions during high-resolution restoration. Motivated by this observation, we revisit Real-ISR from the perspectives of compact latent representation and linear-complexity modeling, and propose SANA-SR, an efficient one-step restoration framework. Specifically, SANA-SR employs a deep compression autoencoder with a 32x compression ratio to drastically reduce latent tokens while preserving restoration-relevant structures and textures. On top of this compact latent space, we introduce a linear-attention DiT with LoRA fine-tuning, enabling efficient high-resolution restoration with linear-complexity token mixing. Extensive experiments on all benchmark datasets demonstrate that SANA-SR achieves highly competitive and often superior quantitative performance against existing methods, while restoring clearer and more realistic textures. Moreover, after pruning, the deployed model runs in 0.019s with 407.95G MACs and 344M parameters, highlighting its strong potential for practical mobile deployment.

Authors: Xiang Lv, Kai Zhao, Qiang Wang, Qiao Mo, Chao Zhou, Mingwen Shao

Abstract
Latent diffusion models have recently achieved remarkable progress in Real-world Image Super-Resolution (Real-SR). However, existing methods consistently exhibit a persistent seesaw effect: enhancing perceptual realism compromises structural fidelity. This effect, we argue, is not intrinsic to diffusion models, but largely stems from the architectural constraints of prevailing two-stage frameworks. To address this challenge, we propose PixelSR, a novel single-stage Pixel Diffusion Transformer for Real-SR that operates directly in pixel space. Specifically, PixelSR consists of two core designs: Pixel-space Conditional Diffusion and Orthogonal Flow Decoupling. The former establishes an effective pixel-space restoration framework by integrating spatially aligned LR conditioning, visual semantic guidance, and lightweight texture refinement, thereby bypassing VAE compression and enabling end-to-end optimization directly in RGB space. Complementarily, the latter decomposes the asymmetric flow target into a low-rank semantic component and a noise-free orthogonal texture component. By routing these components to specialized branches, it achieves decoupled optimization with reduced mutual interference. Benefiting from these designs, our PixelSR restores perceptually realistic details while faithfully preserving structural content. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that PixelSR not only achieves competitive performance, but also effectively mitigates the fidelity--realism seesaw effect.

Abstract
We present Stable Signer V3, an interactive high-resolution sign language avatar system built on 4D Deformable Primitive Splatting. Abstract
One-Step Diffusion Models have demonstrated promising capability and fast inference in video super-resolution (VSR) for real-world. Nevertheless, the substantial model size and high computational cost of Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) limit downstream applications. While low-bit quantization is a common approach for model compression, the effectiveness of quantized models is challenged by the high dynamic range of input latent and diverse layer behaviors. To deal with these challenges, we introduce LSGQuant, a layer-sensitivity guided quantizing approach for one-step diffusion-based real-world VSR. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method has nearly performance to origin model with full-precision and significantly exceeds existing quantization techniques. Time-Correlated Video Bridge Matching
Authors: Viacheslav Vasilev, Arseny Ivanov, Nikita Gushchin, Maria Kovaleva, Alexander Korotin
First: 2025-10-14T12:35:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T13:41:34+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Bridge Matching (BM) learns a stochastic transport between two data distributions and is therefore a natural tool for data-to-data tasks such as video frame interpolation, image-to-video generation, and video super-resolution. Existing bridge models for video, however, use a reference process that treats the frames of a clip independently, so temporal structure enters only through the network or the choice of the bridge endpoints. We propose Time-Correlated Video Bridge Matching (TCVBM), which places temporal structure into the reference process itself: the frames of a clip are coupled by a linear SDE whose drift is a discrete Laplacian along the time axis, and known frames enter as boundary conditions. The transition and bridge distributions remain Gaussian and available in closed form, so training and sampling are as simple as in standard BM, which is recovered in the limit of zero coupling. A spectral analysis shows that the prior damps high temporal frequencies, injects temporally smooth noise, and relaxes towards a boundary-determined profile, such as the linear interpolation between key frames. In controlled comparisons with frame-independent bridges under identical architectures and training budgets, TCVBM improves most automatic metrics for frame interpolation (Moving MNIST, UCF-101, Vimeo-90K) and image-to-video generation (Moving MNIST, UCF-101), e.g., reducing FVD from 688 to 611 for frame interpolation on UCF-101, and human raters prefer its frame interpolations for temporal consistency in 49$\%$ of comparisons versus 15$\%$ for the baseline ($p<10^{-17}$); for super-resolution, it performs on par with BM on automatic metrics.

Bridge Matching (BM) learns a stochastic transport between two data distributions and is therefore a natural tool for data-to-data tasks such as video frame interpolation, image-to-video generation, and video super-resolution.

Abstract

Metric Depth Estimation from Arbitrarily Degraded Low-Resolution Depth Prompts
Authors: Kun Wang, Yun Zhu, Pan Zhou, Na Zhao
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-02-10T08:10:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T09:19:56+00:00
Comments: Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We propose AdaDS, an Adaptive Diffusion Sampling framework for prompted metric depth estimation, which estimates high-resolution metric depth from an image and an arbitrarily degraded low-resolution depth prompt. This setting is commonly studied as depth super-resolution, where existing methods typically regress depth directly without an explicit prior over high-quality depth, and thus often produce artifacts under severe or unseen degradations. In contrast, AdaDS exploits the contraction property of Gaussian smoothing: as noise accumulates in the forward diffusion process, the noisy distributions of degraded prompts and their high-quality counterparts converge toward an isotropic Gaussian prior, while the prompt content is gradually erased. Accordingly, AdaDS refines the prompt and estimates its uncertainty to adaptively select a starting timestep, and injects learned noise to place the intermediate sample in a high-density region of the depth distribution learned by a pre-trained diffusion model. Denoising from this sample yields high-quality depth with fine-grained details and corrects residual errors, even under imperfect prompt refinement. Experiments on real-world and synthetic benchmarks demonstrate its superior zero-shot generalization and robustness to diverse degradations.

VHA: VQA-based Hallucination Assessment for Generative Image Super-Resolution
Authors: Donghun Ryou, Inju Ha, Jaeyoo Park, Bohyung Han
First: 2026-10-19T04:34:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T04:34:57+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://dhryougit.github.io/Hallucination-Assessment-pages/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3 · Project1
Abstract
Generative image super-resolution produces visually realistic high-resolution images, but may also introduce hallucinations by adding unsupported content or altering existing details. Because existing super-resolution metrics fail to measure whether generated details remain grounded in the reference image, we present a hallucination assessment technique based on bidirectional visual question answering (VQA), which we term VQA-based Hallucination Assessment (VHA). The proposed approach uses a vision-language model (VLM) to determine whether the semantic content and low-level visual evidence in a super-resolved image are supported by its ground-truth counterpart. To this end, it constructs question--answer pairs from each image and evaluates them on both images. Discrepancies between the resulting scores in both directions reveal missing or altered ground-truth information and unsupported additions. However, obtaining these scores requires computationally expensive VLM inference. We therefore develop VHA+, a learned, lightweight model that directly predicts VHA scores from ground-truth and super-resolved image pairs. We construct a hallucination benchmark, VHA-bench, through a human study and show that both VHA and VHA+ correlate highly with human judgments of hallucination. The code, benchmark, and model weights are available at https://github.com/dhryougit/Hallucination-Assessment

Abstract
Most existing hyperspectral image super-resolution methods require modifications for different scales, limiting their flexibility in arbitrary-scale reconstruction. 2D Gaussian splatting provides a continuous representation that is compatible with arbitrary-scale super-resolution. Existing methods often rely on rasterization strategies, which may limit flexible spatial modeling. Extending them to hyperspectral image super-resolution remains challenging, as the task requires adaptive spatial reconstruction while preserving spectral fidelity. This paper proposes GaussianHSI, a Gaussian-Splatting-based framework for arbitrary-scale hyperspectral image super-resolution. Authors: Xintong Jiang, Yu Tian, Yixue Liu, Valerio Hoyos-Villegas, Viacheslav Adamchuk, Shangpeng Sun

Abstract
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) enable high-throughput platforms for crop monitoring but face a fundamental trade-off between flight altitude and image spatial resolution. Many existing super-resolution (SR) methods for UAV imagery rely on oversimplified synthetic degradation models and are restricted to fixed integer upsampling scales, limiting their ability to handle real-world degradations and varying flight altitudes. This study proposes MultiAltitudeSR, a framework that conditions feature modulation and reconstruction on source and target ground sampling distances (GSDs). MultiAltitudeSR trained jointly on real UAV images at altitudes of 10 to 70 m above ground level supports all 21 source-to-target altitude pairs without pair-specific fine-tuning, covering scale factors ranging from 1.18$\times$ to 6.03$\times$. Abstract

A Unified Theory of Image Scaling: Point Spread Functions, Discrete Kernels, and Scale-Spaces
Authors: Assaf Shocher
First: 2026-10-15T18:00:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T18:00:06+00:00

Abstract
We derive the optically aligned system of image-scaling operators $\mathbf{K}_s$ that reproduces optical zoom (exact zoom-out, canonical zoom-in), by identifying a Huygens-like consistency principle in which each pixel acts as a secondary source. The system is determined in closed form by the camera's point spread function (PSF), itself the operator $\mathbf{K}_0$. The operators compose, $\mathbf{K}_{s'} \circ \mathbf{K}_s = \mathbf{K}_{ss'}$ (with band projection for down-up), and account for both upscaling and downscaling, which form a Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse pair, $\mathbf{K}_{1/s} = \mathbf{K}_s^\dagger$. We show that image scaling, as routinely practiced, is neither optically faithful nor internally consistent; for example, common anti-aliasing rules generally correspond to no camera, and two resizes by a factor of $1/2$ generally do not equal one resize by a factor of $1/4$. Gaussian scale-space kernels cannot be exact finite-aperture PSFs. Gaussian scale-space is an idealized special case of what emerges here: a generalized scale-space for sampled images, derived entirely from camera optics, bidirectional, and not limited to Gaussian kernels. The system goes beyond scaling and supports composable and invertible subpixel geometric transformations. We empirically validate its optical predictions with real captures and noisy optics simulations, and demonstrate its practical impact on the stability of tasks such as super-resolution and classification.
