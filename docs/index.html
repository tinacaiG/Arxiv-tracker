Time-Correlated Video Bridge Matching
Authors: Viacheslav Vasilev, Arseny Ivanov, Nikita Gushchin, Maria Kovaleva, Alexander Korotin
First: 2025-10-14T12:35:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T13:41:34+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Bridge Matching (BM) learns a stochastic transport between two data distributions and is therefore a natural tool for data-to-data tasks such as video frame interpolation, image-to-video generation, and video super-resolution. Existing bridge models for video, however, use a reference process that treats the frames of a clip independently, so temporal structure enters only through the network or the choice of the bridge endpoints. We propose Time-Correlated Video Bridge Matching (TCVBM), which places temporal structure into the reference process itself: the frames of a clip are coupled by a linear SDE whose drift is a discrete Laplacian along the time axis, and known frames enter as boundary conditions. The transition and bridge distributions remain Gaussian and available in closed form, so training and sampling are as simple as in standard BM, which is recovered in the limit of zero coupling. A spectral analysis shows that the prior damps high temporal frequencies, injects temporally smooth noise, and relaxes towards a boundary-determined profile, such as the linear interpolation between key frames. In controlled comparisons with frame-independent bridges under identical architectures and training budgets, TCVBM improves most automatic metrics for frame interpolation (Moving MNIST, UCF-101, Vimeo-90K) and image-to-video generation (Moving MNIST, UCF-101), e.g., reducing FVD from 688 to 611 for frame interpolation on UCF-101, and human raters prefer its frame interpolations for temporal consistency in 49$\%$ of comparisons versus 15$\%$ for the baseline ($p<10^{-17}$); for super-resolution, it performs on par with BM on automatic metrics.

HP-PnP-Flow: Hybrid Time Scheduling and Projection for Plug-and-Play Image Restoration via Flow Matching
Authors: Yong Li, Xiaojie Wang, Xiaoyan Zhang
First: 2026-10-19T11:15:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T11:15:11+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Image restoration is a fundamental inverse problem that seeks to recover high-quality images from degraded observations. Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods provide a flexible framework for integrating pretrained denoisers into iterative reconstruction, whereas flow matching offers an efficient generative modeling approach based on continuous-time ODE dynamics. Very recently, Martin et al.\ (ICLR, 2025) developed a PnP-Flow method, which integrates the PnP framework with flow matching through a time-dependent denoiser constructed from a pretrained flow matching model.By incorporating a different time schedule and an additional pixel-space projection operator, this paper proposes a novel efficient PnP-Flow algorithm (HP-PnP-Flow) that enjoys rigorous convergence guarantees under mild assumptions, without imposing restrictive conditions on iterates required by the aforementioned work. Numerical experiments on CelebA and AFHQ-Cat demonstrate that the newly proposed HP-PnP-Flow algorithm significantly outperforms PnP-Flow on several restoration tasks such as denoising, deblurring, super-resolution, random inpainting, and box inpainting. Notably, our algorithm achieves comparable or better reconstruction quality with substantially fewer iterations, reducing the iteration count from $500$ to $100$ for deblurring and super-resolution on AFHQ-Cat.

Metric Depth Estimation from Arbitrarily Degraded Low-Resolution Depth Prompts
Authors: Kun Wang, Yun Zhu, Pan Zhou, Na Zhao
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-02-10T08:10:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T09:19:56+00:00
Comments: Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We propose AdaDS, an Adaptive Diffusion Sampling framework for prompted metric depth estimation, which estimates high-resolution metric depth from an image and an arbitrarily degraded low-resolution depth prompt. This setting is commonly studied as depth super-resolution, where existing methods typically regress depth directly without an explicit prior over high-quality depth, and thus often produce artifacts under severe or unseen degradations. In contrast, AdaDS exploits the contraction property of Gaussian smoothing: as noise accumulates in the forward diffusion process, the noisy distributions of degraded prompts and their high-quality counterparts converge toward an isotropic Gaussian prior, while the prompt content is gradually erased. Accordingly, AdaDS refines the prompt and estimates its uncertainty to adaptively select a starting timestep, and injects learned noise to place the intermediate sample in a high-density region of the depth distribution learned by a pre-trained diffusion model. Denoising from this sample yields high-quality depth with fine-grained details and corrects residual errors, even under imperfect prompt refinement. Experiments on real-world and synthetic benchmarks demonstrate its superior zero-shot generalization and robustness to diverse degradations.

VHA: VQA-based Hallucination Assessment for Generative Image Super-Resolution
Authors: Donghun Ryou, Inju Ha, Jaeyoo Park, Bohyung Han
First: 2026-10-19T04:34:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T04:34:57+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://dhryougit.github.io/Hallucination-Assessment-pages/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3 · Project1
Abstract
Generative image super-resolution produces visually realistic high-resolution images, but may also introduce hallucinations by adding unsupported content or altering existing details. Because existing super-resolution metrics fail to measure whether generated details remain grounded in the reference image, we present a hallucination assessment technique based on bidirectional visual question answering (VQA), which we term VQA-based Hallucination Assessment (VHA). The proposed approach uses a vision-language model (VLM) to determine whether the semantic content and low-level visual evidence in a super-resolved image are supported by its ground-truth counterpart. To this end, it constructs question--answer pairs from each image and evaluates them on both images. Discrepancies between the resulting scores in both directions reveal missing or altered ground-truth information and unsupported additions. However, obtaining these scores requires computationally expensive VLM inference. We therefore develop VHA+, a learned, lightweight model that directly predicts VHA scores from ground-truth and super-resolved image pairs. We construct a hallucination benchmark, VHA-bench, through a human study and show that both VHA and VHA+ correlate highly with human judgments of hallucination. The code, benchmark, and model weights are available at https://github.com/dhryougit/Hallucination-Assessment

Voronoi-guided Bilateral 2D Gaussian Splatting for Arbitrary-Scale Hyperspectral Image Super-Resolution
Authors: Jie Zhang, Jinkun You, Shi Chen, Yongshan Zhang, Yicong Zhou
First: 2026-04-20T02:21:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-18T12:57:23+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Most existing hyperspectral image super-resolution methods require modifications for different scales, limiting their flexibility in arbitrary-scale reconstruction. 2D Gaussian splatting provides a continuous representation that is compatible with arbitrary-scale super-resolution. Existing methods often rely on rasterization strategies, which may limit flexible spatial modeling. Extending them to hyperspectral image super-resolution remains challenging, as the task requires adaptive spatial reconstruction while preserving spectral fidelity. This paper proposes GaussianHSI, a Gaussian-Splatting-based framework for arbitrary-scale hyperspectral image super-resolution. We develop a Voronoi-Guided Bilateral 2D Gaussian Splatting for spatial reconstruction. After predicting a set of Gaussian functions to represent the input, it associates each target pixel with relevant Gaussian functions through Voronoi-guided selection. The target pixel is then reconstructed by aggregating the selected Gaussian functions with reference-aware bilateral weighting, which considers both geometric relevance and consistency with low-resolution features. We further introduce a Spectral Detail Enhancement module to improve spectral reconstruction. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of GaussianHSI over state-of-the-art methods for arbitrary-scale hyperspectral image super-resolution.

MultiAltitudeSR: Multi-Altitude Agricultural UAV Image Super-Resolution via Physical Ground Sampling Distance Conditioning

First: 2026-10-18T03:51:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-18T03:51:58+00:00

Abstract
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) enable high-throughput platforms for crop monitoring but face a fundamental trade-off between flight altitude and image spatial resolution. Many existing super-resolution (SR) methods for UAV imagery rely on oversimplified synthetic degradation models and are restricted to fixed integer upsampling scales, limiting their ability to handle real-world degradations and varying flight altitudes. This study proposes MultiAltitudeSR, a framework that conditions feature modulation and reconstruction on source and target ground sampling distances (GSDs). MultiAltitudeSR trained jointly on real UAV images at altitudes of 10 to 70 m above ground level supports all 21 source-to-target altitude pairs without pair-specific fine-tuning, covering scale factors ranging from 1.18$\times$ to 6.03$\times$. The framework can be extended to additional source-target GSD combinations by incorporating corresponding image pairs into training. On the 20-to-10 m pair, the model achieved competitive perceptual performance compared with pair-specific baselines, with LPIPS of 0.2236, DISTS of 0.2431, and NIQE of 8.9020. Experiments using the same architecture demonstrated the value of acquiring real-world multi-altitude UAV image pairs for SR training, with real-pair training achieving better average reconstruction performance across the 21 altitude pairs than training with the tested synthetic degradation pipeline. Downstream zero-shot segmentation case studies illustrated task-dependent benefits of super-resolved imagery for crop monitoring, suggesting potential UAV flight-time savings through image acquisition at higher altitudes.

Abstract
We present Stable Signer V3, an interactive high-resolution sign language avatar system built on 4D Deformable Primitive Splatting. It addresses three limitations of existing avatars: restricted facial expression, inaccurate identity-specific body deformation, and loss of fine appearance details. Shared Face Primitive Splatting adds transferable superquadric primitives for richer expression control; Test-Time Body Primitive Splatting learns an identity-specific virtual muscle layer for more faithful motion; and Test-Time Tunneling Super-Resolution uses TunnelingNet to recover identity-specific high-resolution details. Both test-time modules are optimized once per signer identity; inference then runs entirely from the stored identity parameters. Together, they reduce facial geometry error by 71.4%, body-boundary error by 35.1% across ten identities, and high-resolution RGB reconstruction error by 24.2% on held-out evaluations, while retaining interactive streaming.

Authors: Aditya Rastogi, Ramon Leiser, Prateek Singh, Martha Foltyn-Dumitru, Martin Bendszus, Fabian Bamberg, Klaus Maier-Hein, Kai Schlamp, Alexander Radbruch, Gianluca Brugnara, Philipp Vollmuth

Abstract
Reducing MRI acquisition time improves patient comfort, reduces motion artefacts, and increases scanner throughput. Clinical protocols often shorten acquisitions by sacrificing spatial resolution, particularly along the slice axis. Super-resolution can recover higher-resolution images, but existing methods are typically specialised to one scale or acquisition geometry, limiting their use across heterogeneous clinical protocols. We present GSMR3D, a continuous volumetric model that reconstructs arbitrary isotropic and anisotropic resolutions with a single checkpoint per MRI contrast. It combines a learned dictionary of anisotropic 3D Gaussian prototypes with scale-conditioned frequency encoding and an exact measurement-consistency projection, keeping reconstructions consistent with the acquired low-resolution data. We train separate models on 3,731 contrast-enhanced T1-weighted and 30,254 T1-weighted brain MRI volumes from clinical and population-based cohorts. We evaluate GSMR3D against geometry-specific specialists for isotropic, in-plane, and through-plane super-resolution using reconstruction quality and downstream tasks: brain tumour segmentation, brain-metastasis detection, and brain parcellation. GSMR3D outperforms specialists at isotropic $2\times$ and achieves better LPIPS, DISTS, and FID across all geometries, although specialists retain higher PSNR in anisotropic settings. It more faithfully preserves glioma segmentations across all geometries, achieves the highest lesion sensitivity on an independent brain-metastasis cohort, and the highest brain-parcellation agreement for isotropic and in-plane reconstruction. Reconstruction takes about 6s per volume, adding little overhead relative to acquisition-time savings. GSMR3D offers a flexible approach to MRI scan-time reduction with one model per contrast across geometries while preserving information relevant to downstream tasks.

Abstract
Open-domain video super-resolution (VSR) requires temporally coherent details across widely varying scenes and subjects. However, existing training datasets are often semantically imbalanced and insufficiently diverse. We address this data bottleneck with a structured curation pipeline for open-domain VSR, SCOPE. In this pipeline, we combine task-oriented video selection with hierarchical semantic rebalancing. Applying this pipeline to a large-scale collection of data sources, we construct the SCOPE-VSR dataset for training and SCOPE-test for testing. Abstract
While recent autoregressive video diffusion models achieve remarkable streaming quality, they remain confined to low resolutions (e.g., 480P), leaving efficient, scalable, real-time high-resolution video generation a fundamental open challenge. To bridge this gap, we present Ultra Flash, a cascaded streaming framework capable of real-time high-resolution video generation. Rethinking EEG Spatial Super-Resolution as Dynamic State Decomposition and Filtering
Authors: Hongjun Liu, Leyu Zhou, Chang Ge, Shitong Duan, Chao Yao
First: 2026-10-17T07:41:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T07:41:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
EEG spatial super-resolution aims to recover high-density neural signals from sparse electrode observations. Existing methods have achieved substantial progress through spatial modeling, generative reconstruction, and temporal representation learning. However, the current EEG state contains two different sources of information: predictable evolution inherited from preceding states and newly emerging changes revealed by the current observation. Most approaches typically do not model these two components explicitly. In this work, we formulate streaming EEG spatial super-resolution as a sequential state filtering problem and propose Dynamic State Filtering (DSF). Rather than relying on a single temporal reconstruction pathway, DSF combines a history-aware dynamical branch with an innovation-filtering branch that explicitly decomposes waveform-state estimation into prediction and observation-driven correction. To infer how spatially incomplete innovations should affect missing locations, DSF routes the observed innovations across electrodes through a mask-constrained, topology-aware transition operator and incorporates them through uncertainty-aware state correction. The two complementary estimates are finally fused to reconstruct the dense EEG signal. Experiments on SEED, SEED-IV and BCI2000 demonstrate that DSF improves reconstruction fidelity and downstream utility while maintaining robustness under dynamically changing and unreliable sparse observations.

Abstract
Diffusion magnetic resonance imaging is a noninvasive technique that is sensitive to the motion of water molecules in human tissue. The image signal is commonly described in q-space; however, a complete coverage of this space is often infeasible due to hardware and acquisition time limitations. In this work, we propose to represent the entire diffusion signal using an implicit neural representation network with wavelet activation functions. The signal space is encoded using Fourier features and spherical harmonics basis functions for a continuous output distribution of the raw diffusion data, enabling both super-resolution and q-space interpolation. Additionally, we introduce a pretraining strategy using a group template, enabling faster per-subject optimisation when combined with nonlinear registrations of subjects into template space. We tested the method with 10 subjects from the Human Connectome Project and 26 subjects from in-house data (multidimensional diffusion MRI from a Parkinson's disease cohort). The results show that the proposed method yields high-fidelity reconstructions with significantly reduced memory requirements compared to raw diffusion-weighted images. The pretraining scheme showed good generalisability properties on data acquired with very different protocols. We further assess the method on downstream metrics relevant to diffusion MRI data from the learned representations.

A Unified Theory of Image Scaling: Point Spread Functions, Discrete Kernels, and Scale-Spaces
Authors: Assaf Shocher
First: 2026-10-15T18:00:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T18:00:06+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We derive the optically aligned system of image-scaling operators $\mathbf{K}_s$ that reproduces optical zoom (exact zoom-out, canonical zoom-in), by identifying a Huygens-like consistency principle in which each pixel acts as a secondary source. The system is determined in closed form by the camera's point spread function (PSF), itself the operator $\mathbf{K}_0$. The operators compose, $\mathbf{K}_{s'} \circ \mathbf{K}_s = \mathbf{K}_{ss'}$ (with band projection for down-up), and account for both upscaling and downscaling, which form a Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse pair, $\mathbf{K}_{1/s} = \mathbf{K}_s^\dagger$. We show that image scaling, as routinely practiced, is neither optically faithful nor internally consistent; for example, common anti-aliasing rules generally correspond to no camera, and two resizes by a factor of $1/2$ generally do not equal one resize by a factor of $1/4$. Gaussian scale-space kernels cannot be exact finite-aperture PSFs. Gaussian scale-space is an idealized special case of what emerges here: a generalized scale-space for sampled images, derived entirely from camera optics, bidirectional, and not limited to Gaussian kernels. The system goes beyond scaling and supports composable and invertible subpixel geometric transformations. We empirically validate its optical predictions with real captures and noisy optics simulations, and demonstrate its practical impact on the stability of tasks such as super-resolution and classification.

Topology-Inspired Fine-Tuning for Wind-Field Super-Resolution
Authors: Arjun Dadhwal, Brian Summa
First: 2026-10-15T17:28:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:28:39+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 6 figures; extended version of a poster accepted at TopoInVis Connect 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Scientific super-resolution (SR) models are typically optimized with pointwise reconstruction losses, yet scientific interpretation often hinges on extrema, connected regions, and multiscale scalar structure. We characterize this tension with pretrained CNN and GAN wind-field SR models. Pointwise metrics, bottleneck persistence-diagram (PD) distance, and merge-tree (MT) distance favor the CNN, whereas order-2 Wasserstein PD distances favor the sharper GAN, so even different distances on the same topological descriptor reward different properties. Motivated by this disagreement, we fine-tune the pretrained CNN with differentiable topology-inspired auxiliary losses on scalar speed, gradient magnitude, soft high-speed level sets, and local maxima, and with fixed-pair supervision, which constrains predicted values and persistence at ground-truth persistence-pair endpoints located offline with the Topology ToolKit. A loss study of 16 fine-tuning configurations, each compared with a matched standard fine-tuning control that uses only the original reconstruction loss, on a fixed 168-field benchmark (with topology evaluated on a fixed 160 by 160 subregion of each field), shows that, among the scalar-field losses, gradient supervision provides the strongest PD improvement but does not improve mean MT distance, whereas fixed-pair supervision gives the largest and most consistent joint improvement in PD and MT distances. The final objective combines these two signals. Relative to the pretrained CNN, it lowers mean bottleneck, $W_{2,\infty}$, and $W_{2,2}$ distances by 31%, 25%, and 27% (and by 32-35% relative to the control, which on its own worsens all three), lowers all three on 150 of 168 fields, and gains 1.30 dB PSNR, about half the gain of standard fine-tuning. Mean MT distance also improves, but on only 91 of those 150 fields, indicating that PD and MT capture complementary structure.

Authors: Vignesh Gopakumar, Zhuoyuan Wang, Ander Gray, Valentin Duruisseaux, Josh Williams, William Michael Edward Ellis, Nuno Nobre, Matthew Field, James Panayis, Alexander I. Blair, Andrew Davis, Stanislas Pamela, Robert Akers, Marc Peter Deisenroth, Anima Anandkumar

Abstract
Neural operators approximate solution operators of partial differential equations at a fraction of the cost of numerical solvers. Efficient grid-based architectures such as the Fourier neural operator (FNO) assume uniform rectangular grids, whereas engineering simulations use large, irregular, and locally refined meshes. We introduce L-SplineFNO, a memory-efficient neural operator that couples an FNO backbone with a sparse, learnable tensor-product B-spline transfer between unstructured meshes and a latent Cartesian grid. A local normalised scatter avoids the geometry-dependent solve of analytic spline transformation while preserving compact support, partition of unity, and spline smoothness by construction. The neural operator, conditioned on global design and operating parameters, learns the mapping of geometry to the field solution. Across six three-dimensional engineering benchmarks, L-SplineFNO achieves strong predictive accuracy while requiring substantially less memory and compute than baseline operators, scaling to 26.6 million nodes in training and 46 million in inference on a single A100 GPU. It also supports zero-shot super-resolution and efficient closed-form spatial derivatives. Uniquely, the analytic spline decoder yields rigorous prediction bounds over continuous space and uncertain parameters, via Bernstein convex hulls and Taylor models, demonstrated on a 29.2-million-node MAST-U tokamak model.

Abstract
Modern image super-resolution methods generate detailed, visually appealing results, but they often introduce visual artifacts: unnatural patterns and texture distortions that degrade perceived quality. These defects vary widely in perceptual impact--some are barely noticeable, while others are highly disturbing--yet existing detection methods treat them equally. We propose artifact prominence as an evaluative target, defined as the fraction of viewers who judge a highlighted region to contain a noticeable artifact. We design a crowdsourced annotation protocol and construct SR-Prominence, a dataset suite containing 3,935 artifact masks from DeSRA, Open Images, Urban100, and a realistic no-ground-truth Urban100-HR setting, annotated with prominence. SR-Prominence is released with an objective scoring protocol that allows new metrics to be benchmarked on our suite without further crowdsourcing. Together, the data and protocols enable SR artifact evaluation to move from binary defect presence toward perceptual impact. SR-Prominence is available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/imolodetskikh/sr-artifact-prominence.

PanoDiff-SR: Synthesizing Dental Panoramic Radiographs using Diffusion and Super-resolution
Authors: Sanyam Jain, Bruna Neves de Freitas, Andreas Basse-OConnor, Alexandros Iosifidis, Ruben Pauwels
First: 2025-07-12T09:52:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T08:52:26+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3 · Project1
Abstract
Interest in generating realistic synthetic medical images has grown in recent years. Such datasets can mitigate the scarcity of public data for artificial intelligence research and support education. Work on synthesizing dental panoramic radiographs (PRs) has so far relied almost exclusively on generative adversarial networks (GANs), and three limitations recur: the synthesized field of view is often restricted to a cropped dentoalveolar region rather than a complete PR; observer studies of GAN-generated PRs report that clinicians still identify synthetic images with accuracies of roughly 78-82%, implying persistent visible giveaways; and adversarial training remains sensitive to instability and mode collapse. Generating a full-size PR directly at diagnostic resolution with a diffusion model is an alternative, but sampling at that resolution is costly. We therefore present PanoDiff-SR, an end-to-end two-stage pipeline developed and evaluated specifically for panoramic radiography: a denoising diffusion implicit model (PanoDiff) generates a low-resolution (LR) seed PR ($256\times128$), which a fine-tuned hybrid attention transformer (HAT) super-resolution (SR) stage upscales to a high-resolution (HR) PR of $1024\times512$. Decoupling the expensive generative step from the resolution step yields complete, full-arch PRs at diagnostic resolution on a single 47 GB GPU. The Fréchet inception distance between 7243 real and synthetic HR images is 40.5. Abstract

Abstract
